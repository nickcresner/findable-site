How do I get my plumbing business recommended by ChatGPT?

Short answer: ChatGPT doesn't keep a list of the best plumbers. When someone asks, it usually searches the web and repeats what the top sources say, which for UK trades is mostly directories, review sites and Google listings. So make your details complete and identical everywhere it reads, collect steady real reviews, and check what it says about you. Nobody can guarantee a mention.

  1. Same details everywhere. Business name, phone, website, the areas you cover and your services should match word for word on your Google profile, website and every directory. Mismatches make it harder for any system to be sure they're all you.
  2. Finish your Google Business Profile. Main category "Plumber", every service you do, service areas, opening hours, real photos. Google lists relevance, distance and prominence as the main local factors (Google).
  3. Claim the free listings the other assistants use: Bing Places for Business (Microsoft Copilot) and Apple Business (Siri and Apple Maps). See the table below.
  4. Check the directories AI actually cites for plumbers. In two independent UK tests of ChatGPT-style web answers in Aug and Sep 2026, Checkatrade was cited in 78 of 80 and 81 of 120 answers (Murray Digital; James Tandy). If you're already listed somewhere, make sure the listing is accurate and complete.
  5. Ask every happy customer for a review, with no incentives. Assistants often quote ratings and review counts straight from the listings they read.
  6. Say plainly on your website what you do and where, e.g. "boiler repair in Finchley N3", with your phone number as text, not just in an image.
  7. Check, then recheck. Ask the same questions every month and write down what you see (method below).

Where do AI assistants get information about local plumbers?

Short answer: Each assistant reads different sources. Gemini and Google's AI Overviews lean on Google's index and Google Maps; Microsoft Copilot on Bing and Bing Places; ChatGPT on web search partners plus licensed data such as Yelp; Claude on a web search that independent tests link to Brave Search; Perplexity on its own crawler and many sites; Siri on Apple Maps. Being listed and consistent across all of them is the safe bet.

Do I need schema, llms.txt or a special file for AI?

Short answer: No special file is required. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no extra files or markup, and a 2026 Ahrefs log study found 97% of llms.txt files received no visits at all in a month. Structured data and an llms.txt file are cheap housekeeping, not a switch that gets you recommended.

What matters more is that crawlers are allowed in and your details are written as plain text. Google's guidance is explicit that you "don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup" (Google). Ahrefs looked at 137,000 sites and found most llms.txt files were never fetched (Ahrefs). We still keep both tidy on our own site because they cost nothing, but we don't sell them as a fix.

One thing worth checking: if your website blocks the search crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot, Bingbot, Googlebot), those assistants can't read it.

Should I pay for Checkatrade to show up in AI answers?

Short answer: Not on AI grounds alone. Directories like Checkatrade are cited a lot in AI answers for trades, but no published test shows that paying for a listing makes an individual plumber get named. Judge a paid directory on the jobs and profit it brings you, and keep any listing you already have accurate.

The UK tests that found Checkatrade in most ChatGPT-style answers say so themselves: a directory being cited doesn't prove its members get recommended, and one study couldn't match enough businesses to test it (James Tandy). Murray Digital found ChatGPT named about 1 in 5 of the businesses in Google's map pack, mostly because a list had them (Murray Digital). Free listings are low-risk; 12-month paid contracts deserve the usual maths.

How can I check whether AI recommends my business?

Short answer: Ask each assistant the questions a customer would ask, such as 'Who is the best plumber in Finchley?' and 'I need an emergency plumber in Finchley today', in a private window. Note the date, the assistant, which businesses it named and which sites it cited. Repeat a few times, because answers change between runs, and recheck monthly.

  • Use the same five questions every time, so this month can be compared with last month.
  • Ask once more directly: "What do you know about [your business] in [area]?" If the details are wrong, that's what to fix first.
  • If you have a website, Bing Webmaster Tools now shows when Copilot cites your pages (Bing), and ChatGPT adds utm_source=chatgpt.com to links it sends you (OpenAI).

How long does it take, and can anyone guarantee it?

Short answer: No one can guarantee an AI recommendation, and you should be wary of anyone who does. Assistants that search the web can pick up changes once the sites they read are updated; answers from a model's built-in knowledge only change when it's retrained. Expect to measure over months, not days.

OpenAI and Perplexity say robots.txt changes reach their search systems in about 24 hours (OpenAI; Perplexity), but that is crawl access, not a recommendation. Directory and review changes depend on those sites. We show you the trend each month and never promise positions.
